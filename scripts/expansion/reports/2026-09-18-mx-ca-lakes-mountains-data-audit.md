## Judgment calls for a human

J1. **Lobstick Lake 1,500 km²** (lake-lobstick-lake, 122 views) — the only figure is frwiki's, and frwiki's own infobox flags it: `superficie = 1500`, `superficie notes = Superficie approximative en l'absence de données publiées` ("approximate, in the absence of published data"); enwiki's infobox has `area =` empty and `type = reservoir`. It is one basin of the Smallwood Reservoir system, which is in at 6,527 km² (frwiki lists Ossokmanuan and the Churchill as its neighbours), so the wave now carries a reservoir and one of its parts, with the part's figure a guess that sits above the 1,000 km² line. Lean: drop (Ossokmanuan, the other part, is already out under the views floor), or size it as a range `Lobstick Lake|0-1500`… the engine has no "unknown but big"; if kept, the lead should accept that "larger than 1,000 km²" rests on a figure frwiki itself calls approximate.

J2. **Lake Ainslie 57.4 vs Wikidata 100** (lead-flagged) — resolved in the article's favour by the infobox's own dimensions: `length = 20 km`, `width = 7 km` (≈ 57 km² for a lake this shape), `volume = 0.33 km³` at `depth = 5.75 m` (= 57 km²). Wikidata's 100 is a rounded guess. No range needed.

J3. **Pico del Aguila beside Ajusco** (mountain-pico-del-aguila 3,900 m, 335 views; mountain-ajusco 3,930 m, 639 views — 0.62 km apart, dupes.out). Pico del Aguila is the second summit of the Ajusco volcano (the Ajusco row's 3,930 m is the complex's higher summit, Cruz del Marques from memory — the Ajusco article is not in the wikitext cache); the Monte Rosa-tops precedent of the Europe audit keeps subsidiary summits with their own article. Same shape, further apart: Atwell Peak / Mount Garibaldi (1.07 km), Pyroclastic Peak / Mount Cayley (0.42 km), Ice Peak / Mount Edziza (the complex's two summits), Mount Fay / Mount Babel (Alberta) (0.78 km, two of the Ten Peaks). Keep; two answers for one massif on one round is the standing convention.

J4. **Rockies false positives from the range table's own entries** — Howser Spire (range "Purcell Mountains, East Kootenay") is tagged `the Rockies` because range-tag.mjs's table lists "Purcell Mountains"; the Purcells are the Columbia Mountains, west of the Rocky Mountain Trench, which is the Rockies' western boundary under every definition (Mount Farnham, also a Purcell peak, escaped only because its range field says "Farnham Group"; the pre-wave Selkirk peaks Mount Sir Donald and Mount Sir Sandford are rightly out). Mount Nirvana and Keele Peak (Mackenzie Mountains / Selwyn) are tagged by the table's "Mackenzie Mountains" / "Selwyn Mountains" entries; the Canadian Rockies end at the Liard River on Wikipedia's definition, though some references call the Mackenzies the Rockies' northern extension. The table's comment says "never a tagged foothill" is the acceptable error; Howser Spire is not a foothill. Lean: remove "Purcell Mountains" from the table and Howser Spire from the theme; the Mackenzie pair is the lead's call.

J5. **Mount Petain** (mountain-mount-petain, 3,196 m) — the name was rescinded by Alberta and BC in 2022: the cached infobox reads `name = Unnamed Alberta–BC mountain`, `other_name = Mount Pétain (1919–2022)`, and the description says "Mountain in Canada, formerly Mount Pétain". The article title is still "Mount Pétain" and there is nothing else to type, so the row is the only possible one; the review will show a name that officially no longer exists. Keep.

J6. **Mont Wright** (mountain-mont-wright, size 0, 152 views) — description "Mountain and mine in Quebec, Canada"; the probe's list title was "Mont Wright, Quebec"; the wikitext-cache entry is empty (no `{{Infobox mountain}}`, so no elevation anywhere: "no figure anywhere").
