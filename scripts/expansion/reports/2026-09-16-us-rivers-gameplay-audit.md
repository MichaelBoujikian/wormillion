### C3. A new bare-name river blocks the in-category spelling correction even when the player typed the category's own generic word (check 4/5)

`run.submit`: a fuzzy hit is discarded when `elsewhere(rawInput, category, exactOnly)` finds an exact name in another cohort, and that exact-only pass still strips filler — so "lake meade" → "meade" → the new Meade River (`river-meade`, Alaska, 404 km, 30 views) is "an exact name anywhere", and the correction to Lake Mead is refused.

| typed on | old bank | new bank |
|---|---|---|
| **"Lake Meade"** / "Meade" on a lake round | accepted: Lake Mead (corrected) | `elsewhere` — "Meade is a river – this round wants a lake" |
| **"Lake Vermillion"** / "Vermillion" on a lake round | accepted: Lake Vermilion (corrected) | "Vermillion is a river" (`river-vermillion`, wikiTitle `Vermillion River (Minnesota)`, two L's, 183 views; `river-vermilion`, `Vermilion River (Louisiana)`, is a separate new row) |
| **"Mackinaw Island"** / "Mackinaw" on an island round | accepted: Mackinac Island (corrected) | "Mackinaw is a river" (`river-mackinaw`, `Mackinaw River`, Illinois, 183 views) |
| **"Lake Edwards"** / "Edwards" on a lake round | accepted: Lake Edward (corrected) | "Edwards is a river" (`river-edwards`, `Edwards River (Illinois)`, 30 views) |
| "Greys Peak" / "Greys" on a mountain round | accepted: Grays Peak (corrected) | "Greys is a river" (`river-greys`, `Greys River`, Wyoming, 213 views) |

Meade, Vermillion and Mackinaw are the *common* misspellings of Mead, Vermilion and Mackinac; with the generic word typed there is no ambiguity about what the player meant. The rule ("an exact name anywhere beats a correction anywhere", HANDOFF) was built for "Australia on a Europe round"; here it fires on the loose form of a bare-word river. Fix options, cheapest first: (a) in `run.js` `elsewhere`, when `exactOnly`, skip the loose pass for a candidate whose only claim is the stripped form of an input that carries the *current* category's own filler word (lake/loch/lough on a lake round, island/isle on an island round, mount/peak on a mountain round); (b) aliases `Lake Meade` on Lake Mead, `Lake Vermillion` on Lake Vermilion, `Mackinaw Island` on Mackinac Island, `Lake Edwards` on Lake Edward in `scripts/data-physical.mjs` — an exact alias in the cohort wins before the fuzzy pass runs (validate must accept them; "Vermillion" bare would collide with the river's loose form only if made an alias, so keep the "Lake" in it). The bare typings ("Meade", "Mackinaw") are a judgment: those *are* real rivers.

## Judgment calls

- **J1. "Creek", "Bayou", "Fork", "Branch", "Run" are not filler**, so they feed every letter rule: 159 new "X Creek", 70 "X Fork", 13 bayous, 11 branches. "Name a river that ends in K" grew from 52 to 284 answers (166 of the 232 new only through the word Creek/Fork), "with a double letter" 299 → 690 (115 only through the "ee"), "with a K in it" 355 → 728 (201 only via Creek/Fork), "with a long name" 459 → 684 (90 only via the generic word: "Buffalo Bayou", "Wounded Knee Creek"). ★ for "ends in K" and "double letter" is now Jack Creek (12 views). Consistent with "entries are named as the world names them" and with how "Toms River" counts as a short city name; but if the house rule is "filler words are not letters", `creek`/`bayou`/`fork`/`branch`/`run` belong in `matching.FILLER` — which also changes the matcher ("Teche" would then find Bayou Teche, see J3; and the loose forms of "Rock Creek" and the river "Rock" would collide, which `validate` refuses, so the 159 creeks would need re-checking). The user's call; not undone here.
- **J2. Two prompts newly drawable at exactly `MIN_ELIGIBLE` = 6**: "Name a river that ends in F" (Taff, Chelif, Wolf, Portneuf, Leaf, Boeuf) and "Name a river that ends in P" (Kamp, Loup, Puyallup, Swakop, Tonle Sap River, Deep). The threshold working as designed; flagged because these are the only two prompts the wave brought into existence.
- **J3. "Bayou X" rows have no bare alias**: "Teche", "Lafourche", "Macon", "Meto" on a river round are `unrecognized` ("Bartholomew" is nudged to Bartolomé Island). "Bayou Teche" is how people say it, so probably fine; aliases `Teche`, `Lafourche` in `scripts/data-physical.mjs` would cost nothing.
- **J4. "D" typed alone is accepted as the D River** (its letter variant is the single letter `d`), including on "Name a river that starts with D." Harmless and a little funny; so is "the D". The Roe behaves normally.
- **J5. "Chama" on "starts with C" is refused** (the row is "Rio Chama"; `rio` is a letter where it is the name — the documented Rio Grande rule). Consistent; noted because Americans say "Chama River" as often as "Rio Chama". An alias would not change the letter rule (convenience aliases never feed it).
