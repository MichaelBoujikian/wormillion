## Method

- 13 lists tried, 11 exist: the cities of China (by population and plain,
  the prefecture-level divisions), Japan (plain and by population — 913
  links, every city), South Korea, North Korea, Taiwan, Mongolia, Hong
  Kong; `listCountry` per list, then **every row's country re-read from
  its description** (the South Korea list carries the North's cities; the
  China list carries Taipei). 3,176 candidates → 1,490 read as cities; 116
  present, 7 taken, 79 fuzzy, 771 missing; 456 under 50,000, 61 with no
  figure.
- Chinese prefecture-level cities carry the whole prefecture's population
  on Wikidata (Ganzhou 8.97 M, Jining 8.36 M…) — the "administrative city"
  rule of `data-cities.mjs` (Chongqing's 30 M) accepts it; 58 new rows are
  over 5 M, 241 over 1 M. County-level cities (Yiwu, Kunshan's neighbours)
  are in; districts, rural townships and towns are not.
- Views floor 91: six rows under it left out.

## For a human

- **The city jackpot share went 1.38% → 4.87%.** The line did not move
  (312.6 views); 187 new rows — small Japanese cities of 50–100k and Chinese
  county-level cities — sit in the 91–312 band. The 91-views floor is the
  standing rule (the Mexico decision), and every row is a real city over
  50,000, so they are in; the knob is a higher city floor for this wave
  (213 views would drop 133 of them and put the share near 2%). A call
  for the user, not made here.
- Chinese prefecture-level figures are the prefecture's (the bank's
  convention since Chongqing); the review will say "Ganzhou, 8,970,014"
  for a city whose urban core is a tenth of that. The audit is asked to
  flag any figure that crosses 5 M / 10 M wrongly.
- Gwangju, Suzhou and Fuzhou stay bare beside their qualified namesakes
  (bare titles; the standing rule) — "Suzhou" typed is Jiangsu's.
