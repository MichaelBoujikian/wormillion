## Confirmed

### Check 2 — right figure

Thresholds actually in `SIZE_RULES.river` (`src/js/promptBank.js`) are **500 / 1,000 / 3,000 / 5,000 km** (the brief's 1,000/3,000 omit the 500), inclusive both ways (`satisfiesSize`: `hi >= value` / `lo <= value`). Of the 949 rows: 425 have Wikidata and the infobox agreeing, 277 are infobox-only, 61 are infobox-over-Wikidata disagreements (all eyeballed, all plausible, none crossing a threshold except North Canadian below), 174 are Wikidata-only, 14 (the famous short rivers) were sized by hand. 22 rows are ≥ 500 km, 3 ≥ 1,000 km. The cohort now has 613 rivers ≥ 500 km and 230 ≥ 1,000 km.

1. **Herbert Run** — `river-herbert-run`, `scripts/data-physical.mjs:3837` `Herbert Run|665`, 30 views. The infobox says `| length_mi = .413` (0.413 mi = **0.66 km**; a small stream into the Patapsco in Baltimore County). `scripts/expansion/article-size.mjs` `amountOf()` matches `/([\d][\d,.]*)/`, which needs a leading digit, so `.413` was read as `413` mi → 665 km. The row is 1,000× too long, sits above the 500 km threshold, and is under the wave's own 80 km floor, so it should not exist at all. **Player sees:** "Name a river longer than 500 km" accepts "Herbert Run"; "shorter than 500 km" refuses it with "Herbert Run doesn't fit that pattern" (verified through `run.submit`). It is not the ★ reveal for either (Seal, 9 views, is rarer). **Fix:** `node scripts/expansion/drop.mjs river-herbert-run`; and in `article-size.mjs` let both numeric regexes accept a leading dot (`/(\d[\d,.]*|\.\d+)/`). It is the only leading-dot length in the whole wikitext cache, so no other row is affected.
2. **Fourche Maline** — `river-fourche-maline`, `data-physical.mjs:3833` `Fourche Maline|1127`, 30 views, `wikiTitle: Fourche Maline (Oklahoma stream)`. No infobox figure; Wikidata P2043 = **1127 km**, reference "imported from French Wikipedia" (P143 → Q8447, hash `d4bd87b8…`). It is a tributary of the Poteau River (itself 227 km) impounded by Wister Lake; frwiki has 112,7 km and the import lost the decimal. **Player sees:** "longer than 1,000 km" and "longer than 500 km" accept "Fourche Maline" (verified); not the ★ (Chulym, 23 views, is rarer on the 1,000 prompt). **Fix:** `Fourche Maline|113` in `data-physical.mjs`, or drop the row if a figure that only frwiki carries is not acceptable under the sourced-figure rule.
3. **Munuscong** — `river-munuscong`, `data-physical.mjs:3872` `Munuscong|512`, 30 views. Infobox `| length = ` is empty; Wikidata P2043 = **512 km**, the same frwiki import (same reference hash). It is a short Upper Peninsula river into Munuscong Lake (St. Marys River); frwiki has 51,2 km. **Player sees:** accepted for "longer than 500 km", refused for "shorter than 500 km" (verified). **Fix:** `Munuscong|51`, which also puts it under the 80 km floor — so drop it.

Tooling note: 41 of the 174 Wikidata-only rows carry that frwiki-import reference; every integer value ≥ 500 among them was decimal-shifted (2 of 2), while the ones under 500 that I could sanity-check (Belle Fourche 470, Satilla 418, Catawba 350, Yadkin 346, Ogeechee 370, Oconee 274, Withlacoochee 227…) are right. A cheap guard for `probe.mjs`/`article-size.mjs`: when the only figure is a Wikidata claim referenced P143 = Q8447 and it would clear a size threshold, refuse it unless the infobox or prose agrees. (`audit-wd-claims.json` has every row's claims and references.)

### Check 3 — right name

4. **San Francisco** — `river-san-francisco`, `data-physical.mjs:3585` `San Francisco|256`, 122 views, `wikiTitle: San Francisco River (Gila River tributary)`. The bare name is the Spanish/English spelling of the **São Francisco** (`river-sao-francisco`, 2,914 km, 1,339 views, a top-ten South American river; es.wikipedia titles it "Río San Francisco"). Through `run.submit`: on "Name a river in South America", "San Francisco" / "Rio San Francisco" / "San Francisco River" were a nudge ("San Francisco is a city") and are now **"San Francisco isn't in South America"** — the false-scope message the brief warns about; on a plain river round they were the same nudge and are now accepted and scored on the 122-view Arizona river (a jackpot-tier rarity for a player who meant the Brazilian one). "Sao Francisco"/"São Francisco" still land correctly. **Fix:** `node scripts/expansion/drop.mjs river-san-francisco` (a 256 km tributary at 122 views is not worth the trap); optionally then give São Francisco the alias `San Francisco` (`Sao Francisco|2914|San Francisco` at `data-physical.mjs:829`) so the Spanish spelling is accepted rather than nudged to the city.
5. **McKinley** — `river-mckinley`, `data-physical.mjs:3864` `McKinley|93`, 30 views, `wikiTitle: McKinley River` (Alaska). Because "mount" is filler, **"Mount McKinley" typed on a river round is now accepted as the McKinley River** (verified: old bank nudged "Denali is a mountain"; new bank `accepted:McKinley [30v]`), i.e. a category slip on the most famous name in Alaska scores as one of the rarest rivers in the cohort. "McKinley" on a mountain round still lands on Denali (no change). **Fix:** drop `river-mckinley` (93 km, 30 views, 13 km over the floor); the filler rule itself is a decision not to undo.

### Check 3 — right name

Naming form: 945 of 949 names are exactly `chunk.mjs`'s form of the title (parenthetical off, "River" off unless the bare name is a state or country — hence "Iowa River", "Michigan River", "Florida River", "Turkey River"); the four deviations are deliberate ("Ocoee" for `Toccoa/Ocoee River` with aliases Toccoa / Ocoee River / Toccoa River; "Chateauguay" accent-folded; "D River" and "East River" keeping their generic word because "D" and "East" are not names). Every new name, its "X River" form, the stripped title and every alias is `accepted` on its own row in the new bank (only the literal string "Toccoa/Ocoee River" is unrecognized, and nobody types that). The fork/branch rows land from their natural long forms ("Salt Fork of the Red River", "North Fork of the Flathead River", "West Branch of the Susquehanna River" — of/the/river are filler). The five re-pointed rows (Fox, Current, McKenzie, Rainy, Brandywine) keep their names and aliases, now resolve to the right article with US coordinates, and their views jumped (Fox 61 → 2,922, Current 61 → 1,157, Rainy 30 → 761, McKenzie 30 → 1,005, Brandywine 30 → 944); the Dong keeps "Dong River"/"Dongjiang" and "East River" now lands on its own 8,706-view row. These are real rivers named as the world names them and the house style is generosity, so most are fine; the 30-view ones (San Jose, Little Rock) turn a capital-name slip into a jackpot-tier river and are worth a look alongside McKinley.
2. **Corrections to a famous river that a new exact name now pre-empts** ("an exact name anywhere beats a correction anywhere"): on "Name a river in Europe", "Weiser" was accepted as the Weser and is now **"Weiser isn't in Europe"** (`river-weiser`, 166 km, 91 views — "Weiser" is a natural misspelling of Weser); likewise "Lumber" → Humber, "Boone" → Boyne, "Cache" → Canche, "Middle" → Piddle; on "longer than 1,000 km", "Pease" was the Peace and is now "Pease doesn't fit"; on a plain round "Rouge" was corrected to the Rogue (2,222 views) and is now the River Rouge (Michigan, 1,096). Only Weiser/Weser and Rouge/Rogue are typos people actually make; the rest were lucky corrections. This is the documented cost of the exact-beats-correction rule, not a data error; if the lead wants to soften it, dropping Weiser is the one cheap move.
3. **Other categories' rounds where an old correction became a river nudge**: city round "Harlem" was accepted as Haarlem (a common misspelling of the Dutch city) and is now "Harlem is a river"; capital round "Sheyenne" was Cheyenne and is now "Sheyenne is a river"; city round "Hillsborough" was Hillsboro; lake round "Edwards" was Lake Edward; island round "Saline" was Salina. The rest of the 25 such cases (Suiattle→Seattle, Queets→Queens, Cannon→Guangzhou, Susan→Sudan, Buckland→Auckland, New Fork→New York City…) were corrections nobody wanted and are improvements.
