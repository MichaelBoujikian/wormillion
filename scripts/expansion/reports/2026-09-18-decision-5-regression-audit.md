### C2 (low–medium, engine) — a bare loose form now scores a newly folded obscure namesake instead of the famous place that holds the form through an alias
- Evidence (`check4b.out`, working tree vs main, plain rounds through `try.mjs`): "Cook" on a mountain round → Mount Cook (Canada), 152 views, 81% obscure (main: Aoraki, 8,858, via its alias "Mount Cook"); "San Antonio" → San Antonio Mountain (New Mexico), 274 views, 74% obscure (main: Mount Baldy (California), 4,049, alias "Mount San Antonio"); "Morrison" → Mount Morrison (California), 335 (main: Yushan, 2,831, alias "Mount Morrison"); "Holy" on an island round → Holy Island (Anglesey), 2,161 (main: Lindisfarne, 27,487, alias "Holy Island"); "White" → White Island (Brooklyn), 335 (main: Whakaari / White Island, 7,823); "Rabbit" → Rabbit Islands, 213 (main: Ōkunoshima, 3,835, alias "Rabbit Island"); "North" on a river round → North River (West Virginia), 30 views, 84% obscure (main: Bei, 183, alias "North River"). The full forms are right: "Mount Cook" → Aoraki, "Mount San Antonio" → Mount Baldy, "Holy Island" → Lindisfarne, "White Island" → Whakaari, "Rabbit Island" → Ōkunoshima, "North River" → Bei (the exact key is a list, most-viewed first). These seven are every loose form in the bank (3,817 with no exact owner) whose resolution changed against main to a less-viewed holder; two more ("long" → Long Mountain over Longs Peak's alias, "arabian" → Arabian Sea over the Persian Gulf's alias) were already so on main and are the rule working as intended.
- Cause: `matching.resolveLoose` — a form claimed by an entry's NAME beats the same form from another entry's alias (the v1.2 Arabian rule, SPEC 3.7). Decision 5 folded namesake NAMES for forms that a famous entry had held only through an alias, so the name-holders now take the form outright and the most-viewed-first ordering never sees the alias-holder.
- What the player sees: "San Antonio" on "Name a mountain" (what a Southern Californian types for Mount Baldy) silently scores a 274-view New Mexico volcano at 74% obscurity — a free jackpot from an ambiguous typing, the thing the design set out not to do; "Cook" likewise, at 81%.
- Fix (engine, `src/js/matching.js` `resolveLoose`): when a form has both name-holders and alias-holders, let an alias-holder lead the list when its views exceed every name-holder's by `NAMESAKE_FAME_RATIO` (3×) — Arabian stays the sea (the gulf has 1.6×), the seven above flip to the famous place (6× to 58×). Or accept and note it in SPEC 3.7 (the sentence "A form that comes from an entry's NAME beats the same form from another entry's alias" now has these seven consequences). A data fix is not available: the aliases are right and the new rows are right.

### N1 (note, not a finding) — the three "regressions" of check 3 are the length rule judging the typed spelling
`Avon (Warwickshire)`, `Scamander` and `White Desert (Egypt)` on "Name a river/desert with a long name" were accepted on main because the old parenthetical name counted as 16–24 letters (the design brief called that a bug); typed bare now they are refused as short, and their long aliases ("Warwickshire Avon", "Karamenderes", "White Desert Protected Area") are accepted — the same as Philippines / Taiwan / Micronesia, which qualify for "long" only through "Republic of the Philippines" etc. By design (SPEC 3.1a / v1.2 length rule).
