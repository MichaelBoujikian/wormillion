## Confirmed findings

### F1. Mulligan (river-mulligan / "Mulligan River") is in Queensland, Australia — wrong place, wrong theme
- What is wrong: the Canada chunk row `Mulligan|340||Mulligan River|North America` (work/folded/mx-ca/new-rivers-ca.txt:288) folded the enwiki article "Mulligan River", which is the Channel Country river in Queensland. The link on "List of rivers of Newfoundland and Labrador" resolves to the Australian article (no cache here knows an enwiki article for a Labrador Mulligan; not checkable offline).
- Evidence: work/ca-rivers.json item "Mulligan River": description "River in Queensland, Australia", sources ["list"], lists ["List of rivers of Newfoundland and Labrador"], status "fuzzy" (matched Mullica). scripts/.cache/titles.json "Mulligan River": lat -24.9625, lon 138.586. work/wikitext-cache.json "Mulligan River": `subdivision_name1 = [[Queensland]]`, `subdivision_name2 = [[Channel Country]]`, length 340 km. src/data/rivers.json river-mulligan lat -24.9625 lon 138.586; src/data/themes.js:990 lists Mulligan under North America.
- What the player sees: "Mulligan" is accepted on a "river in North America" round for an Australian river (and would be a legitimate answer nowhere else - there is no Oceania river theme to put it in).
- Fix: **drop** river-mulligan (scripts/data-physical.mjs:5546 `Mulligan|340`, scripts/data-wiki-titles.mjs:7431, src/data/themes.js:990); `drop.mjs`. (Re-pointing is not possible offline: no enwiki article for the Labrador Mulligan is known to the cache.)
- Severity: wrong place (and a wrong theme acceptance).


### F2. Blue Creek (Belize) (river-blue-creek-belize / "Blue Creek (Belize)") — the article is a Maya archaeological site, not a river
- What is wrong: the enwiki article "Blue Creek (Belize)" carries `{{Infobox historic site}}` ("The ruins excavated at the Maya site 'Blue Creek'", part_of Orange Walk District); its 122 views/mo are the ruins'. The 87 km figure is not from that article: work/mx-rivers-sizes.json says `eswiki "Río Azul (América Central)" (longitud: 87 km)` — the Wikidata item Q4929007 sitelinks the enwiki site article to eswiki's river article, so the sister-wiki pass sized a ruin as a river.
- Evidence: work/wikitext-cache.json "Blue Creek (Belize)" (`{{Infobox historic site`, caption "The ruins excavated at the Maya site"); work/mx-rivers.json item: description "River and archaeological site in Belize", sources ["list"] (List of rivers of Mexico), status "taken"; src/data/rivers.json river-blue-creek-belize size 87, magnitude 122, lat 17.9 lon -88.9.
- What the player sees: "Blue Creek" on a North America river round is accepted and the review reveals a Maya ruin's article; on a plain river round the Belize row (122 views) outranks Idaho's (15 views) as the most-viewed namesake, so a bare "Blue Creek" now scores on the ruin.
- Fix: **drop** river-blue-creek-belize (scripts/data-physical.mjs:5242 `Blue Creek (Belize)|87`, scripts/data-wiki-titles.mjs:7136, src/data/themes.js:928). If the lead wants Belize's Rio Azul it needs a river article (not checkable offline whether enwiki has one; "Rio Azul" is also a Guatemalan Maya site). Blue Creek (Idaho) then stays a qualified sole holder (the Grey (New Zealand) precedent).
- Severity: wrong place (the article is not a river).

### F3. Three Canada rows sit on opposite sides of the 500 km line depending on the source — Peribonka, Beaver (Alberta), Ekwan
- What is wrong: the lead's rule took the enwiki infobox figure; Wikidata's figure (recorded in the same sizes file) is across the "longer / shorter than 500 km" line. The engine supports a `lo-hi` range for exactly this ("Amur|2824-4444" in scripts/data-physical.mjs:1272; `satisfiesSize` in src/js/promptBank.js:321 takes either end), and none of the three carries one.
  - **Peribonka** (river-peribonka / "Peribonka River"): bank 451; work/ca-rivers-sizes.json `article 451 vs wikidata 547 (length_km: 451)`; wikitext-cache `length_km = 451`, `length_ref = Atlas of Canada`. scripts/data-physical.mjs:5444 `Peribonka|451`.
  - **Beaver (Alberta)** (river-beaver-alberta / "Beaver River (Canada)"): bank 491; sizes `article 491 vs wikidata 689 (length: {{cvt|491|km}})`. scripts/data-physical.mjs:5634 `Beaver (Alberta)|491`.
  - **Ekwan** (river-ekwan / "Ekwan River"): bank 500; sizes `wikidata 585, article 500`, "how" says "article (wikidata agrees)" although the two differ by 85 km (the agreement test is looser than the threshold). At exactly 500 the inclusive rule already answers both directions, so this one is harmless today; listed for the record. scripts/data-physical.mjs:5415 `Ekwan|500`.
- What the player sees: "Name a river longer than 500 km" refuses Peribonka and Beaver, whose Wikidata (and the frwiki-side sources behind it) call 547 and 689 km; a player who knows the longer figure is told it is wrong.
- Fix: **re-size to a range** — `Peribonka|451-547`, `Beaver (Alberta)|491-689` (and, for consistency, `Ekwan|500-585`) in scripts/data-physical.mjs, then build-data. Not checkable offline which figure is "right"; the range is the engine's own answer to a two-source disagreement.
- Severity: crosses a size threshold (Peribonka, Beaver); cosmetic (Ekwan).

### F4. "George River" scores St. George River (Maine) and "Smoky River" scores Smoky Hill River — the loose index outranks an entry whose exact name is the stripped form
- What is wrong: the two rows went in bare "beside a loose holder" (the Canada report: George 565 km beside St. George's loose "george", Smoky 492 km beside Smoky Hill). In `matchAnswer` (src/js/matching.js, the loose pass) `lookup.loose.get(bare)` is consulted before `lookup.get(bare)`, and `buildLookup` never registers a loose claim for a name whose loose form equals its own key (`if (!bare || bare === key) return;`). So the loose form "george" is held by St. George alone, and "george river" (bare "george") settles there, never reaching the entry literally named George.
- Evidence (try.mjs, plain river round, working tree): `George` → accepted:river-george (335 views); `George River` → accepted:river-st-george (122 views); `Smoky` → accepted:river-smoky (244); `Smoky River` → accepted:river-smoky-hill (731). river-george and river-smoky have no aliases (src/data/rivers.json). The scan `loose-shadow.mjs` (beside this report) finds 8 such bare river names on the working tree, 7 on d577de5: the class pre-exists (Hondo, Puerco, Maurice, Black Bayou, Middle, Saint Charles; the old St. Mary is now qualified) and this wave adds George and Smoky.
- What the player sees: typing the George River the natural way ("George River") on a plain or North America round scores a 122-view Maine river instead of Quebec's 565 km one; "Smoky River" scores Smoky Hill (Kansas). Both are accepted, so the player never learns the swap, but the review names the wrong river and the rarity points are the wrong row's. (On a round the shadow does not fit — "longer than 500 km", where St. George's 108 km is out — the subset lookup has no St. George and "George River" does reach George: verified with try.mjs.)
- Fix: **engine** (preferred, fixes all eight): in `matchAnswer` try `lookup.get(bare)` — an entry whose exact name is the stripped form — before, or merged into the list with, `lookup.loose.get(bare)`; or register such names as `fromName` claims in `buildLookup` so the shared loose form resolves to the list and the scope/most-viewed pick applies (decision 5's own rule). **Data** (immediate, this wave only): aliases `George River` on river-george and `Smoky River` on river-smoky in scripts/data-physical.mjs (`add-alias.mjs`).
- Severity: wrong acceptance (the wrong row is scored on every round where the shadow also fits — verified on the plain and North America rounds).

### F5. "Mountain River", "Desert River", "Fond du Lac River", "Bay du Nord River" are unrecognized — the name carries another category's generic word
- What is wrong: four new rows are named with a word `WORD_CATEGORY` assigns to another cohort (mountain, desert, lac, bay). Typing the row's own Wikipedia form "X River" trips `foreignWordIn` (src/js/matching.js:81), which refuses the loose pass, and no exact key "x river" exists because the bank name is bare. The bare form works only because it is an exact key. The incumbent River Isle shows the bank's own remedy: river-isle carries aliases ["River Isle","Isle River"].
  - river-mountain "Mountain" (Mountain River (Northwest Territories), 357 km, 91 views): `Mountain River` → unrecognized.
  - river-desert "Desert" (Désert River, Quebec, 95 km): `Desert River` / `Désert River` → unrecognized.
  - river-fond-du-lac "Fond du Lac" (Fond du Lac River (Saskatchewan), 277 km): `Fond du Lac River` → unrecognized ("lac" is a lake word).
  - river-bay-du-nord "Bay du Nord" (Bay du Nord River, 80 km): `Bay du Nord River` → unrecognized ("bay" is a sea word).
- Evidence: try.mjs plain river round and North America round (battery.json beside this report): the four "X River" forms are `unrecognized` on both, the bare forms `accepted`. Old stack: the rows did not exist.
- What the player sees: "Mountain River" (the only way most people would type a river called Mountain) is refused as not a river; same for the other three.
- Fix: **aliases** in scripts/data-physical.mjs — `Mountain River` on river-mountain, `Desert River` (and `Riviere Desert`) on river-desert, `Fond du Lac River` on river-fond-du-lac, `Bay du Nord River` on river-bay-du-nord (`add-alias.mjs`). (An engine alternative — treat a foreign generic word as filler when the remainder is an exact name — is broader than this wave needs.)
- Severity: wrong acceptance (a real place refused under its own title).

### F6. "Ha! Ha!" / "Ha! Ha! River" are unrecognized — normalize() keeps the exclamation mark
- What is wrong: the row is named "Ha Ha" (river-ha-ha, wikiTitle "Ha! Ha! River (Saguenay River tributary)", 1,583 views/mo — the most-viewed row of the whole wave). The Canada report says "the matcher keeps '!'" as if that were harmless; it is the opposite: `normalize` (src/js/matching.js) strips `.,'()` and hyphens but not `!`, so the typed Wikipedia spelling keys as "ha! ha!" and matches nothing; the row has no alias.
- Evidence: try.mjs plain and North America rounds: `Ha Ha` → accepted:river-ha-ha; `Ha Ha River` → accepted; `Ha! Ha!` → unrecognized; `Ha! Ha! River` → unrecognized. src/data/rivers.json river-ha-ha aliases [].
- What the player sees: the river's own name, as Wikipedia and every map spell it, is refused.
- Fix: **engine** (preferred): add `!` (and `?`) to the punctuation `normalize` strips (`.replace(/[.,'()]/g, '')` → `/[.,'()!?]/g`) — no other row in the bank uses "!" so nothing else moves; or **alias** `Ha! Ha! River` on river-ha-ha (exact key "ha! ha! river", loose "ha! ha!"), which covers both typed forms.
- Severity: wrong acceptance (a real place refused under its own title); the highest-viewed row of the wave.

### F7. Ausable (river-ausable / "Ausable River (Lake Huron)") takes "AuSable" / "Ausable River" away from the Michigan Au Sable, 8.6× more viewed
- What is wrong: the new bare row "Ausable" (Ontario, 240 km, 244 views/mo) keys as "ausable" — which is also the everyday one-word spelling of the incumbent Au Sable River (Michigan) (river-au-sable, 2,100 views/mo; "AuSable River Canoe Marathon") and the spelling of the Adirondack Ausable River (AuSable Chasm; not in the bank). Decision 5 does not catch it because the two bank names differ by a space ("au sable" vs "ausable"), so no qualifier was asked for and the fold saw a free name. This is the brief's trap class: a bare newcomer whose name belongs to a far more famous river.
- Evidence (try.mjs, plain river round): working tree — `Ausable`, `AuSable`, `Ausable River`, `AuSable River` → accepted:river-ausable (244 views); `Au Sable` → river-au-sable (2,100). d577de5 — the same four inputs → accepted:river-au-sable (corrected) (2,100 views). So a correction to the famous river became a silent acceptance of the obscure one.
- What the player sees: typing the Michigan river its usual way scores a 244-view Ontario river; the review names the wrong river and the rarity points are the Ontario row's (62% obscure instead of 39%).
- Fix: **re-qualify** — make the pair namesakes by hand: rename the Ontario row `Ausable (Ontario)` and give river-au-sable the alias `AuSable River` (or qualify it `Au Sable (Michigan)` and add the alias), so the shared key "ausable" resolves to the list and the most-viewed (Michigan) wins on plain and North America rounds, Ontario's answering only its qualified form — exactly how Thames (Ontario) sits beside the Thames. Whether the validator accepts an alias equal to a qualified namesake's bare name is for the lead to check (it does for decision-5 sets). The New York Ausable, if it is ever added, joins the same set.
- Severity: wrong acceptance (the wrong row is scored; a pre-wave correction to the famous river is lost).
