# United States × rivers — coverage probe and fill (2026-09-16)

The first chunk of the country-by-country scouring (HANDOFF.md: US → Western
Europe → …, all categories per country). Method: every article under
`Category:Rivers of the United States by state` (53 state categories, no
county trees) plus every link on the 50 state list pages ("List of rivers of
Alabama" …) and the two national lists — 20,230 candidate titles, 12,717
resolved to river/stream/creek articles — pushed through the game's matcher
on the river cohort and checked against each entry's `wikiTitle`. New in this
probe: a **length floor of 80 km (≈50 mi)**, with Wikidata's P2043 fetched
first and, because Wikidata has no length for 10,845 of the articles, every
article's infobox read by `article-size.mjs` (the article wins when the two
disagree by more than 15%; 90 disagreements, all resolved to the article).
Anything under the floor but with **1,000+ views a month** was lifted back in
(`famousViews`). Config: `probes/us-rivers.json`; tooling commit `e559f40`.

## Decisions taken on the spot

- **Floor 80 km.** The bank's US rivers had a median of 274 km and a 10th
  percentile of 85 km before the fill; 80 km keeps every river a state list
  bothers to describe and cuts the 7,000 creeks. The next wave can lower it.
- **Famous-below-floor at 1,000 views/mo.** The Mystic (11 km) is an answer;
  a 40 km creek with 30 views is not, yet.
- **Same-name second rivers stay out.** Green River (Kentucky, 618 km, 1,796
  views), Fox River (Green Bay tributary, 2,222 views), Grand River
  (Michigan, 2,679), White River (Indiana, 2,192), Black River (Arkansas–
  Missouri, 791 vs Jamaica's 213), Santa Cruz (Arizona, 1,126 vs
  Argentina's 335)… would need Wikipedia's parenthetical as their name
  (only reachable by typing it, or by the fuzzy pass) — the same rules
  decision as the 45 name-taken US cities. Listed in `work/us-rivers.report.txt`
  under NAME TAKEN (194 in total, most of them small namesakes).
- **Bare names that are English words** (Big, Bad, Elk, New, Sun, Little,
  Middle, South, Spring, Wind…) went in as the world names them; the matcher
  gives ≤ 4-letter names no fuzzy slack, so they are exact-only.
- **"Salt Fork Red", "North Fork Flathead", "West Branch Susquehanna"**: the
  title minus "River". Typing "Salt Fork of the Red River" lands (of/the/river
  are filler).
- **Toccoa/Ocoee River** → "Ocoee" with aliases Toccoa, Ocoee River, Toccoa
  River.

## Judgment calls left for a human

- The two "shortest river" claimants, Roe (61 m) and D (37 m), are in with
  sizes 0.06 and 0.04 km. Real, famous for exactly that; a size prompt never
  reaches them.
- 7,133 articles have no length anywhere. Most are small, but a spot check
  found real rivers among them (Pacolet River's infobox says "50 m" — a typo
  for mi). A future pass could take Wikidata P2043 from other-language
  wikis, or prose.
- "Miami" (the Florida river, 9 km, 2,253 views) is now a river answer, so
  "Miami" on a river round is accepted rather than nudged toward the city.
  Same for "Bronx", "Harlem", "Calumet".

## What the tooling learned

- Wikidata's length is missing for 85% of US stream articles and wrong (miles
  as km, or a different river) for ~15% of the rest; the article's infobox is
  the source of truth. `article-size.mjs` reads it (convert templates, plain
  "N mi", `length_mi`/`length_km` fields).
- The probe now memoises Wikidata sizes per QID and views per title inside
  its cache, so a re-run after a config change fetches only what is new
  (the first re-run refetched 25 minutes of Wikidata because the batch
  boundaries had shifted).
- Wikipedia throttled every run from the first request (`429` / "too many
  requests" text); the backoff copes, at roughly a request every 5 s. A full
  state-tree probe is ~1 h, the article pass ~1 h, a views pass over 4,000
  titles ~1 h. Never two at once.
